  Anthropic's IPO prospectus leaked through Reuters, and the numbers are huge everywhere you look. The listing could value the company at more than $2 trillion, and it has committed more than $500 billion to data centers and compute before it even goes public. That money is locked in either way.
  
  The detail that caught my eye is something most people skipped past. Anthropic is famously an enterprise company, not a consumer one, and enterprise contracts are the foundation of its revenue. Yet the filing says many of its biggest customers aren't locked into long-term contracts and could cut their spending. If you're an investor, that should make you pause. To be fair, the people buying AI right now don't know what next month looks like, so it makes sense they wouldn't sign multi-year deals.
  
  So why is this fine for Anthropic? My bet is that their long game isn't selling AI to companies at all. It's building models good enough to do valuable work themselves, like medical science and drug development, so that even if customers drift toward cheaper open-source models, Anthropic has another way to make money. It's a very unusual way to go to market, but nothing about this cycle is usual. What did you notice?
